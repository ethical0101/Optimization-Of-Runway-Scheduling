# Abstract

Runway sequencing under mixed arrivals and departures is a safety-critical optimization problem with direct impact on airport delay, controller workload, and throughput. This work presents AERIS-OPT (Adaptive Efficiency Runway Intelligence Scheduler), a graph-driven optimization framework that combines neural priority estimation, separation-aware scheduling, and post-optimization refinement. To align with the base reference study, we reproduce the result-table structure and evaluation protocol using `flight_data.csv`, including objective-function comparison (Table 3), optimized runway allocation details (Table 4), and per-flight delay-cost comparison against FCFS and genetic algorithm baselines (Table 5).

The proposed pipeline enforces wake-separation feasibility while optimizing delay cost in CNY under strict operational targets. In the latest run, AERIS-OPT achieves a delay cost of 37472.9 CNY, schedule window of 900 s, and controller displacement of 1.8333, satisfying all configured strict targets and outperforming FCFS, GA, and MILP on delay cost. Relative to FCFS, delay-cost reduction is approximately 69.25%, while throughput improves from 0.02424 to 0.02667 flights/s. These results indicate that graph-informed sequencing with explicit safety constraints can deliver both operational feasibility and substantial efficiency gains in multi-runway scheduling.
